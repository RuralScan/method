## Question this module answers

Module 5 (M5) answers: what can this nature-based solution plausibly do for the development problems found in the opportunity space, how strongly, how surely, where does the effect land, and what is its rough cost shape? [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §2]

M5 is qualitative. It renders evidence-derived classes. It does not quantify benefits or returns, does not model ecosystem services, and is not cost-benefit analysis (CBA); those are feasibility work, pointed to in Module 6 ([[ch:m6-handoff]]). It does not decide where a practice works (M1) or where to prioritise (M4), and it does not assess investment risk (M2b). [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §2] [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §Scope]

## Inputs

| Input | Table | What M5 reads |
|---|---|---|
| Effect rows | [[table:T6]] | One row per practice × T5 priority: `effect_direction`, `effect_confidence`, `evidence_level`, `agreement_level`, mechanism, conditionality, `applicability`, optional `magnitude_summary`, evidence IDs, traceable account. |
| Hazard responses | [[table:T3]] | Livelihood-mitigation rows per practice × hazard × farming system: `mitigation_potential`, `confidence`, `landscape_scale_only`, `applicability`. |
| Indicative cost profile | [[table:T6E]] | Establishment and recurrent cost and cost-effectiveness denominators, with `economic_value_range` where its gates pass. |
| Problem distribution | M3 | Share of opportunity-space land at each severity level per priority. |
| Display rules | lookups | Rows, chip scale, confidence badges, effect locus, row tags. |

[src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §3] [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §1] [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile]

Effect rows are not written by hand. They are **generated by cell synthesis**: every evidence unit that hits a cell (practice × priority for T6; practice × hazard × farming system for T3) is pooled and reconciled deterministically into one row per applicability scope. [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked] [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §2.1] How a class, its confidence and its transfer class are produced is described in [[ch:effect-synthesis]]; how costs are gated is described in [[ch:cost-profiles]]. This chapter covers how the results are presented.

T6 effect rows link only to T5 `priority` rows. Descriptors (population, market access, farm size, production value) carry no effect row because "what does the practice do to it" does not apply. [src: methodology/opportunity_space_T5.md §mcda_role - priority vs descriptor] [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile]

## Method

### Step 1. One row per problem, in fixed order

The scorecard shows **one row per problem**: the problem's spatial presence (the T5 need layer, computed at run time in M3) beside how strongly this practice responds (the evidence row for that problem). There are no theme chips and there is no practice-level number. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring]

The rows, their panels, their order and the T6 rows each one reads are fixed in a lookup:

{{csv:schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv|cols=row_id,panel_label,label,sources,order}}

Two panels result. "Risk to rural livelihoods (climate)" holds erosion, drought, flood and heat; each reads the T6 row for that hazard priority, which records livelihood resilience to the hazard. "What the NbS can address" holds biodiversity loss, carbon removals, rural poverty, gender inequality, food insecurity and water-table depletion. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv]

The carbon row means removals in soil and biomass, not a "sequestration deficit". Where a practice's carbon evidence is mostly avoided emissions, a per-practice tag says so on the chip (see Step 5). [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv] [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_row_tags.csv]

The scorecard build uses only practice-level, global rows: rows with no suitability family and no applicability scope. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §_global]

### Step 2. Map each evidence class to a chip

Each row's class becomes a chip glyph through a fixed scale:

{{csv:schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv|cols=field,class,glyph,rank,label}}

The glyph matches the evidence class one to one: + slight, ++ moderate, +++ strong, with −, −− and −−− for harm. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv] [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] The sign is in the **benefit frame**: positive means the practice improves the concern (reduces a "higher is more concern" variable). It is reached from the direction of the outcome as measured through the crosswalk's polarity. [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile]

For T3 hazard rows the scale has a separate mapping. T3 `very_high` and `high` both show +++, because `very_high` is `high` at high confidence and the confidence mark already shows that difference; T3 `negative` covers slight and moderate harm. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv]

Three special chips carry states that a scale point would hide:

- **± conflicting evidence.** Material weight lies on both signs. The engine states the conflict instead of averaging it into a near-tie class, and `no_relationship` is reserved for evidence that reports a genuine null, never for cancellation. [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile]
- **? data deficient.** Evidence exists but cannot set a class. A row whose account is marked data deficient, or whose class is not on the scale, shows ? and no confidence mark. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §chip]
- **n/a not assessed.** No evidence row exists for this practice. This is never shown as 0, because absent evidence is not evidence of no effect. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv] [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §11]

### Step 3. Show confidence separately from the symbol

Confidence comes from the IPCC two-axis method: an evidence level (limited, medium, robust) and an agreement level (low, medium, high) combine into five confidence levels through a fixed matrix. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.4]

{{csv:schema/lookups/ipcc_confidence_matrix.csv}}

The scorecard shows confidence **beside** the chip, never inside it, so that size and certainty cannot be read as one thing. Five levels collapse to three marks:

{{csv:schema/lookups/scorecard_confidence_badge.csv}}

[src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] Keeping the two apart matters because the classes are ordinal and a weak class can carry high confidence. Such a pairing means "confidently small", not "strongly true". [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv] [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_confidence_badge.csv]

Transferability is not folded into confidence. Confidence answers "how sure is the literature"; the row's `applicability.transfer_class` (`in_context`, `adjacent`, `out_of_context`, `mixed`) answers "does it apply here". [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.4] For an AOI, the run picks the most specific matching scope row and recomputes the transfer class against the AOI. If the result is the global row and it is `out_of_context`, the scorecard shows a gap (`no_applicable_evidence`, with the row greyed and a statement such as "evidence from high-income temperate contexts only"), not the class. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §5.5] Evidence from outside the target context is therefore always labelled, never silently applied.

### Step 4. Pool rows that combine several T6 rows

A display row may draw on more than one T6 row. At the pin only one does: water-table depletion pools water stress, catchment water yield and water quality. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv] The pooled chip follows the same rule the engine uses inside a cell, applied one level up: [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §theme_chip]

```formula
S = { source rows that are assessed, not data deficient, with confidence ≥ medium }
W+ = Σ w_r over r in S with rank_r > 0
W− = Σ w_r over r in S with rank_r < 0
W± = Σ w_r over r in S with class = conflicting_evidence
dissent = (W± + min(W+, W−)) / (W+ + W− + W±)
if dissent ≥ 1/3: chip = ±
else: chip = weighted median of rank_r over r in S
confidence = lowest confidence among r in S
```

`w_r` is the weight of source row `r` (equal weights of 1 at the pin); `rank_r` is its class on the −3 to +3 scale. If no source row has evidence the chip is n/a; if rows exist but none reaches medium confidence, it is ?. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §theme_chip] [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §build_nbs]

The pooling is a display convenience for one problem. The underlying split remains: on-farm water stress and catchment water yield are separate T5 priorities because trees can improve the first while reducing the second, and they are never averaged into one class in the evidence tables. [src: schema/T5_opportunity_space.csv] Each pooled chip lists its source rows so the reader can see them.

### Step 5. Attach locus, directness, caution and tags

Each assessed chip carries small qualifiers.

**Effect locus: where the effect lands.** The values are on-farm, landscape or downstream, both, or public good. The default comes from the engine's per-practice effect locus (`off_site` shown as landscape, `mixed` as both). A per-problem override wins over it, and a practice-specific override wins over a general one. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §load_locus] [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §locus_for]

{{csv:schema/lookups/effect_locus.csv|cols=nbs_id,suitability_family_id,effect_locus}}

{{csv:schema/lookups/scorecard_locus.csv|cols=nbs_id,row_id,locus,rationale}}

The overrides express physical reasoning. Carbon removal is a global public good with no farm-level beneficiary. Recharge and biodiversity gains accrue across a catchment or landscape. Water-harvesting flood benefits are measured downstream of the works. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_locus.csv] Forest restoration is off-site by default because it does not occur on farmland; plantation forestry is an exception, where grower income accrues on the plot. [src: schema/lookups/effect_locus.csv] Effect locus also marks T3 rows `landscape_scale_only`. [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked] Most practice-level defaults in the engine's locus table are recorded as pending ratification at the pin; the per-problem overrides are recorded as ratified. [src: schema/lookups/effect_locus.csv] [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_locus.csv]

**Directness.** A row is "direct" when at least half of its evidence units measured the outcome itself through a direct crosswalk route. It is "indirect" when most units measured a stand-in or a mechanism, such as income for poverty, canopy cooling for heat or runoff for flood. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §directness] Proxy and component routes already count at 0.7 weight inside the synthesis. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.8]

**Caution.** The chip is outlined when the row's account lists measured harm for some adopters, the maladaptation signal. A minority of harm findings is never hidden inside an agreement score. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §chip] [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked]

**Row tags** qualify a row for one practice:

{{csv:schema/lookups/scorecard_row_tags.csv|cols=nbs_id,row_id,tag,rationale}}

### Step 6. Pair response with problem presence

Join each row to M3's problem distribution, so the reader sees "how much of the opportunity space has this problem" beside "how strongly the practice responds". [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §6] A strong response to a problem that is barely present in the AOI is a weak argument for the practice there, and the pairing makes that visible without combining the two into a score.

### Step 7. Optional filter for the hotspot map

The build also lists the priorities the practice is evidenced to improve: a positive class with a medium or high confidence mark. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §build_nbs] By default the scorecard and the hotspot map are shown side by side. The user may switch on a filter that keeps only these priorities in the hotspot view. T6 never multiplies or alters the hotspot weights. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] See [[ch:m4-hotspots]].

## The indicative cost profile

Cost rows are kept out of the scorecard and written to [[table:T6E]], with the same columns and generator, because they feed the economic profile rather than the effect chips. [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile] Indicator types include establishment cost, recurrent cost, cost per hectare restored, cost per beneficiary, cost per farmer reached and cost reduction. Cost reduction counts as a benefit only where it is a farm-level production-cost saving. Cost per tonne of CO2e avoided is a feasibility-level figure and is not generated. [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile]

These profiles are **indicative and ordinal**: "big or small given the context", not cost-benefit analysis. A numeric `economic_value_range` is emitted only when every gate passes:

- at least two independent sources;
- the same unit;
- the same income band;
- the same scope row.

High-income cost figures are excluded outright from low- and lower-middle-income and global rows. There is no inflation adjustment; the source note records currency years, and low and high are the observed extremes. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.7] Where a gate fails, the profile says there is insufficient evidence; it never borrows a figure. The derivation is covered in [[ch:cost-profiles]].

The module specification describes a richer "economic archetype" card (cost band, time to first income, revenue streams, financing archetype). At the pin the schema carries only an optional archetype identifier on T6, and the module's implementation notes say to render whatever denominators the recipe populated and never compute new ones. [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §6] [src: schema/spec.md §T6 - NbS Scorecard - Effects and Economic Profile] [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §10. Implementation notes (Python)] Treat the archetype card as specified but not supplied by the evidence tables.

## What is deliberately not shown

| Left off | Why |
|---|---|
| A practice-level score or index | It would mix "how big" with "how sure" and treat ordinal classes as equally spaced; a scoping tool should not rank practices on a false-precision number. The build emits no practice-level number. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] |
| Theme chips | They were removed so the view shows one row per problem. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] |
| Fire | Fire is a project risk handled in M2b, not an opportunity-space row. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] |
| Production gap | An intermediate step towards rural poverty and food insecurity, not a row of its own. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv] |
| Asset-threat rows of T3 | They describe damage to the investment and feed the M2b screen; the scorecard excludes them. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §build_nbs] |
| Costs on the effect chips | Costs sit in the separate T6E profile. |
| Magnitudes as the headline | A `magnitude_summary` (median, low, high, n) appears only when at least two sources share a metric and unit, and sits under the class with its n. It is never the value the MCDA uses. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.6] |
| Frost | Frost is out of T3 for every practice. [src: schema/spec.md §T3 - NbS × Hazard × Farming System] |
| Family and scope rows in the chip | The chip reads the practice roll-up. A roll-up row flags `family_spread` when family classes differ by two or more ranks, and the scope rows remain for drill-down and AOI resolution. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §2.2] |
| Agricultural dependency and IPLC lands | These are T5 priorities with no scorecard row at the pin. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv] |

Qualitative-only families, such as household rooftop harvesting and constructed treatment wetlands, are parked out of the generated T3 and T6 tables; their evidence stays in the register for Module 6. [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked]

## Comparing practices

The comparison view uses the **same rows across practices**, one column per practice. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §module docstring] [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §6] For each practice the build also counts its rows as benefit, harm, no effect, or a special state (conflicting, data deficient, not assessed). [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §build_nbs] Read a comparison with these rules:

1. **Compare chip with chip, and confidence with confidence.** A ++ at low confidence and a + at high confidence are different statements; neither outranks the other.
2. **Read the locus.** An on-farm poverty effect and a landscape water effect benefit different people. Avoided damage that accrues to society is kept out of the poverty priority at synthesis. [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked]
3. **Check applicability for the AOI.** A chip that is `out_of_context` for this country is a gap, whatever its class elsewhere. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §5.5]
4. **Compare footprints only within a spatial product type.** A km² of area suitability is never ranked against an applicability-zone surface or a linear product; comparisons across types carry an explicit caveat. [src: methodology/T4_generation_method.md §2.4]
5. **Surface safeguards in the comparison.** When the run found IPLC land overlapping the opportunity space, the comparison block carries the FPIC and ESS7 caveat, not only the hand-off. [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §10]
6. **Do not add chips across rows.** Rows are different benefit streams with different beneficiaries; their sum has no meaning.

## Assumptions and limits

- **What is implemented.** The scorecard assembly (rows, chips, pooling, locus, directness, caution) is implemented and builds from the generated tables. The problem-presence pairing with M3 and the per-AOI transfer recomputation are specified but depend on modules not implemented at the pin. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §build] [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §5.5]
- **The module specification lags the lookups.** It describes a five-point scale (++ to −−) and three confidence levels. The pinned lookups use seven classes plus three special states, and five confidence levels shown as three marks. The lookups and the build are authoritative. [src: methodology/modules/M5_scorecard.md §11] [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_chip_scale.csv]
- **The scorecard climate rows read T6 hazard rows, not T3.** The four climate chips read T6 rows for the hazard priorities. T3 livelihood-mitigation rows, which are resolved by farming system, feed M2 and can be read per practice beside the scorecard. The chip scale for T3 exists, but no display row reads T3 at the pin. [src: schema/lookups/scorecard_rows.csv] [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/runtime/scorecard.py §_source_chip]
- **Fragility.** Many generated classes rest on few sources; removing one unit can change a class or drop a row below the evidence floor. [src: src/nbs_ruralscan/recipe/cell_synthesis.py §meets_evidence_floor] A chip is a summary of the literature as extracted, not a validated prediction for a site.
- **Evidence counts are not strength.** The number of units or sources behind a chip describes effort and coverage. Strength and confidence come only from the synthesis rules.
- **Literature bias.** Published and grey evidence tend to over-report benefits. The synthesis discounts grey literature on effect claims and adds a maladaptation signal, but cannot recover studies that were never published. [src: methodology/T3_T6_generation_method.md §7.8] [src: AGENTS.md §What is locked]
- **What feasibility must still do:** quantify the effects that matter for the investment case at the chosen sites, carry out a full CBA, and test the conditionality noted on each row.
