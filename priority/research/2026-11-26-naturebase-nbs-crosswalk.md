# RuralScan NbS ↔ Naturebase pathway assessment

26 November 2026. Research crosswalk requested by Pete. This is an analyst interpretation of intervention compatibility, not accepted T4/T6 evidence, a new binding, or permission to substitute Naturebase effects into recipes.

## Main finding

Naturebase is a useful **practice-specific climate-mitigation opportunity comparator** for parts of four RuralScan groups: agroforestry, forest restoration, mangrove restoration and wetland management. Riparian buffers have only component-level analogues. Water harvesting has no dedicated pathway, although the conservation-tillage/mulching subpractice overlaps agricultural soil-carbon management.

Its layers describe potential mitigation under specified interventions and counterfactuals. They do not measure present carbon loss or replace RuralScan's suitability, hydrological benefits, livelihood benefits, disaster risk or infrastructure work. Naturebase is developed collaboratively by TNC and partners; its natural climate solutions focus is narrower than the full NbS framework. [Naturebase overview](https://www.naturebase.org/about)

## Six-practice comparison

| Our NbS | Closest Naturebase pathway | Match and useful application | Main gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agroforestry | Cropland-based Agroforestry; Savanna-based Agroforestry | Strong for additional trees in crops and planted silvopasture; broad carbon-opportunity context | Family/crop-specific fluxes, existing-tree retention, linear geometry and on-farm regeneration mechanisms |
| Forest restoration | Reforestation | Strong for natural recovery on eligible land; conditional for planting | No automatic equivalence for enrichment in existing forests, plantations or afforestation of naturally open ecosystems |
| Mangrove restoration | Coastal Wetland Restoration, mangrove component | Strong intervention match; carbon-opportunity comparator | Separate tidal marshes; verify local restoration extent and hydrology |
| Wetland management/restoration | Peatland Restoration; Improved Peatland Management; coastal tidal-marsh restoration | Strong for corresponding peat/coastal subsets | Freshwater mineral wetlands, floodplain reconnection, wetland creation and treatment wetlands |
| Riparian buffers | Conditional woody analogue in reforestation or cropland agroforestry | Partial; contextual carbon comparison only | No dedicated corridor layer, herbaceous-buffer effect or buffer-width representation |
| Water harvesting/conservation | Increased Soil Carbon in Croplands for conservation tillage/mulching only | Limited subpractice overlap | Ponds, check dams, sand dams, runoff capture, terraces and bunds have no dedicated pathway |

The table is our inference from current FAM definitions and the inspected producer pages below. “Strong” means compatible intervention concept, not proven numerical transferability or Haiti coverage.

## What detailed producer pages establish

- **Cropland agroforestry v3:** includes alley cropping, hedgerows, parklands and shaded perennial systems. Carbon rates are averaged across relevant systems within climate zones, with broader fallbacks where evidence is missing. Additional tree-cover opportunity is constrained by agricultural production and an additional forest-suitability filter. It is not a coffee-, cacao-, FMNR- or shelterbelt-specific response map. [Pathway 12](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/12)
- **Savanna agroforestry v3:** adds trees to grazing land with little tree cover; includes silvopasture, hedgerow and parkland analogues. This matches planted silvopasture better than retention of existing trees or unmanaged woodland grazing. [Pathway 11](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/11)
- **Reforestation:** uses natural-regrowth biomass sequestration averaged over the first 30 years, adjusted for albedo and additionality. Excludes existing forests, croplands and wetland/peat settings among other constraints. Thus its title accommodates planting, but its carbon model is not a planting-versus-regeneration comparison. [Pathway 4](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/4)
- **Coastal restoration v3:** combines mangrove and tidal-marsh components. Mangrove accounting includes aboveground biomass, roots and soils, plus avoided remaining soil emissions. Its mapped restoration opportunity excludes some historical losses and cannot represent every locally restorable site. This is a useful BGB-inclusive potential estimate, not observed root-carbon loss. [Pathway 16](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/16)
- **Peat restoration v3:** represents avoided peat emissions through rewetting and applies a non-cropland allocation. Agricultural peat is handled separately. [Pathway 17](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/17)
- **Improved peat management v3:** represents partial rewetting in agricultural peat, specifically a modelled 10 cm rise in annual water table. It is not interchangeable with full restoration or a dedicated paludiculture response. [Pathway 30](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/30)

The live catalogue also confirms that Climate Smart Forestry currently represents reduced-impact logging, whereas the general explanatory website describes a wider family of possible management actions. Use the specific dataset definition. Likewise the detailed coastal product covers mangroves and tidal marshes; the broad website's mention of seagrass does not establish a seagrass raster in this product. [Live data catalogue](https://app.naturebase.org/data), [general science page](https://www.naturebase.org/science-and-data)

## Important gaps and non-equivalences

Naturebase's additional pathways are not missing RuralScan requirements by default. Grassland restoration/protection, grazing feed additives, nutrient management, rice methane management, woodfuel-demand reduction and savanna fire management extend beyond our six current groups. Do not expand scope merely to match its catalogue.

Avoided forest conversion can inform a separately approved protection action; it is not the mitigation effect of planting trees. Avoided coastal/peat impacts likewise describe protection, not restoration. The same pixel can have several plausible actions: one physical intervention cannot earn several full pathway benefits by attaching multiple family labels.

Missing Naturebase correspondence means **not represented by this product**, not no carbon benefit. It particularly misses many of our water, soil-retention and narrow-corridor functions.

## Appropriate use in RuralScan

1. Keep Naturebase as a separate mitigation-potential descriptor/comparator for matching practices. The carbon-loss concern continues its OpenLandMap/CTrees investigation; potential and present loss remain separate quantities.
2. Route comparisons to M3 descriptive context, M5 carbon-benefit interpretation and M6 follow-up as appropriate. No automatic T6 effect substitution and no Naturebase mask silently replaces T4 suitability.
3. Use the appropriate component and local opportunity footprint. A 1 km pixel is not one hectare of eligible implementation. Do not multiply a fraction-weighted raster by opportunity area without establishing its denominator, or apply the fractional area weighting twice.
4. Preserve linear and applicability-zone reporting. Naturebase explicitly cautions against using its implementation-area estimates for pathways finer than 1 km. Riparian buffers, hedgerows and windbreaks therefore require locally derived physical footprints before any defensible benefit calculation.
5. Respect pathway hierarchy and overlap. All-pathway, biome and action totals cannot serve as a specific NbS benefit and are not simple sums of independent layers. Keep carbon pools, non-CO2 gases, time horizon and counterfactual explicit.
6. Catalogue licensing is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Record it and assess compatibility with intended project use; free download is not evidence of unrestricted reuse.

The 2035 accounting horizon shown on reviewed pages is distinct from the 30-year regrowth average used in reforestation. Those are different concepts; neither warrants automatic multiplication of an annual rate by 30. [Reforestation metadata](https://app.naturebase.org/data/nb/4)
