# Newer food-insecurity sources for Haiti

Review date: 27 November 2026. **Newer sources exist. Prefer the latest national IPC acute analysis for current severity; pursue CNSA's ENSSAN 2025 for nationally grounded food-access evidence.** These answer different questions. The existing 2015 chronic IPC source remains an unsuitable current default without renewed applicability evidence. No source or class replacement is activated here.

## What was actually checked

**FEWS NET:** downloaded the [October GeoJSON](https://fdw.fews.net/api/ipcphasemap/country/HT/2026-10-01.geojson). Nine features represent three phase multipolygons in each of three scenarios, not nine independent assessment areas. All geometries are valid. Each scenario covers about **98.6824% of the pinned LSIB boundary**; the remainder is unknown under this boundary comparison. Small cross-phase overlaps exist: 1.1396 km² in CS and ML1, 0.9010 km² in ML2. These must be flagged/reconciled before any non-overlapping area summary; no arbitrary priority or maximum-phase repair is applied. October's CS period ends 31 October: it is not a November observation. ML1/ML2 are projections and must never be stacked as extra observations. Source phase, assistance flag, reporting date, validity interval and provenance identifiers are retained. `false` in the assistance flag is not proof of absence of aid.

**ENSSAN:** printed pp.21–23 describe the sample and strata; pp.27–28 describe collection and limitations. Figure 11 (printed pp.99–100) shows three FIES groups by stratum. Table 39 and §5.6 (printed p.116; PDF page 117) give four national household categories. Neither supplies four published geographic priority classes. Regional aggregation, security-related substitutions and recall require qualification. Scoring/anchors and weighting need verification before borrowing cutoffs; the report's household thresholds are not adopted as universal FAO or area thresholds. Next acquisition target is the weighted stratum table, uncertainty, recall period and matching polygons/IDs, not digitized chart estimates.

**FAO/WDI:** both [Haiti observations](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/HTI/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?format=json&per_page=100) and [indicator metadata](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/SN.ITK.MSFI.ZS?format=json) were downloaded and hashed. The indicator concerns moderate-or-severe food-access insecurity; it is distinct from IPC Phase 3+ and from the separate undernourishment indicator. The returned metadata do not settle the averaging interval. A national prevalence must remain national context, not be painted across Haiti as artificial local variation.

The [audit folder](food-alternatives-audit-20261127/README.md) records exact inputs and inspection outputs. No restricted microdata download, IPC API application, email or new agreement was submitted.

## Recommended next binding decision

For a current acute map, use the latest **national IPC** analysis when its exact geography is available; FEWS NET is an inspected provisional alternative if its different provenance is acceptable. A possible four-display-class proposal is acute Phase 1 → Very little, 2 → Low, 3 → Medium, 4/5 → High, retaining Phase 5 as an explicit critical flag. **This is a project proposal, not a published four-band IPC classification and not approved.** Preserve all five native phases regardless of display. Missing/unanalysed areas remain Not assessed.

For longer-term food-access context, pursue **ENSSAN 2025 weighted subnational estimates**, with DIEM round 6 as a second national-coverage survey route. Use FAO/WDI as qualified national context where needed. Do not silently mix chronic IPC, acute IPC, FIES prevalence and early-warning risk as one metric. Source choice must follow the intended food-insecurity meaning, not recency alone.
