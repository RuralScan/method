1. Evidence behind the Rural NbS Scan

The Rural NbS Scan is a World Bank-funded project helping project teams scope where nature-based solutions could work in rural, agricultural and forestry landscapes, and which investment priorities they could support.

This dashboard shows the research behind that work: which sources were selected, what evidence was extracted, how it was checked, and how it supports the scan’s rules. It supports project oversight and technical review. It does not establish site-level feasibility or replace engineering studies.

1.1 How the Rural Scan works

  1. Define the questionSelect the area, practices and project priorities. A shared framework sets the steps; each practice has its own recipe.
  2. Find the opportunity spaceCombine practice requirements with spatial data to identify suitable or applicable locations, with host-system gates and hard exclusions kept explicit.
  3. Assess risk and prioritiesDistinguish risk to rural livelihoods from risk to the proposed investment. Examine who and what lies in the opportunity space, then identify priority hotspots.
  4. Compare and hand overCompare expected benefits, limitations and evidence gaps. Use the results to guide subsequent feasibility work and local validation.

6. Methods

Understand the Rural Scan, how evidence is prepared for it, and the searches actually recorded. Method descriptions, evidence approval and implementation readiness are separate parts of the record.

6.1 Rural Scan method

The Rural Scan is a scoping method for nature-based solutions in rural landscapes. A common framework defines the analytical steps. Each practice recipe specifies its relevant families, variables, relationships and contextual limits. The runtime combines that configuration with spatial data for a chosen area.

Table 6.1. Analytical stages and the questions they answer
ModuleQuestionResult
M0 · Setup and scopeWhat area, practices and decisions are in scope?A declared study area, context and configuration.
M1 · Opportunity spaceWhere could the practice be suitable or applicable?Practice-family suitability or applicability, with host gates and exclusions.
M2 · Rural climate riskWhere are rural livelihoods exposed to climate hazards?Risk information for understanding need and priorities.
M2b · Project riskWhat hazards or operational barriers could affect the investment?Separate risk filters and flags, not another term added to suitability.
M3 · CharacterisationWho and what lies within the opportunity space?A profile of people, production, environment and other project concerns.
M4 · Priority hotspotsWhere do the chosen project priorities concentrate?Priorities within the opportunity space, with explicit assumptions.
M5 · ComparisonWhat benefits and limitations does each practice offer?Evidence-based response and benefit profiles with uncertainty.
M6 · Hand-offWhat must be checked before implementation?Feasibility questions, local validation needs and next-step guidance.

The scan does not establish site-level feasibility or provide engineering design. Linear practices are not automatically reported as planted pixel area. Evidence-backed configuration still requires appropriate datasets, target-context checks and a tested spatial implementation.

6.2 Evidence workflow

Read the Evidence methods section as one sequence: understand the records and sources, prepare and review claims, then combine eligible evidence using the method for its target table.

6.2.1 Shared evidence methods

  1. Evidence records and source appraisal explains provenance, scientific basis versus extraction format, WOCAT accounts and expert judgement reported in literature.
  2. Discovery → acquisition → extraction → review covers how a source becomes a checked claim.
  3. Combining evidence into analytical tables covers compatibility, repeated observations, shared datasets and author groups, weighting, uncertainty and the synthesis decision.

6.2.2 Methods for each output

  • Agent-led suitability synthesis (T4): AI proposals from reviewed evidence, scientific approval and validation of accepted mappings.
  • Hazard and scorecard effects (T3/T6): eligible outcome claims, direction, magnitude, confidence and transfer.
  • Economic profiles (T6E): cost classes, denominators, context and the limits of monetary ranges.

The other tables need justified configuration choices rather than the same claim-pooling algorithm: practice and scope (T0), datasets and geographic context (T1/T7), hazards (T2), and priorities (T5).
